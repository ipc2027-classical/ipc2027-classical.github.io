We are delighted to announce the classical (deterministic) tracks of the 2027
International Planning Competition (IPC 2027). The competition is organized
in the context of the International Conference on Automated Planning and
Scheduling (ICAPS) and will conclude at ICAPS 2027.

The IPC empirically evaluates state-of-the-art planning systems on a range of
benchmark problems. Its goals are to promote planning research, highlight
challenges for the planning community, and provide new and interesting
benchmarks for future research.

Note: we are still looking for new, interesting domains for the competition!
If you would like to contribute a domain, or know someone who might have an
interesting planning problem, please get in touch with us -- see the Call
for Domains on our website.


PDDL fragment
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

IPC 2027 will use the same subset of PDDL 3.1 as the recent classical tracks
(as since IPC 2011): STRIPS, action costs, negative preconditions, and
conditional effects, possibly in combination with universally quantified
effects (forall). We will also consider domains with disjunctive
preconditions, existential quantifiers, and axioms.


Tracks
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

There will be four tracks:

 - optimal:
       as usual: planners must find optimal plans for as many problems as
       possible. Single CPU core, 8 GB memory, 30 minutes per task. The
       score of a planner is the number of tasks it solves optimally.
       Suboptimal plans are treated like invalid plans (see below).

 - agile:
       as usual: planners must find plans as quickly as possible, regardless
       of their quality. Single CPU core, 8 GB memory, 5 minutes per task.
       A task solved in T seconds scores 1 if T <= 1, 1 - log(T)/log(300)
       if 1 < T <= 300, and 0 otherwise; the score of a planner is the sum
       of its scores over all tasks.

 - plan-improvement:
       similar to the satisficing track, but an initial plan is provided
       for each task. Planners must return a plan of strictly lower cost;
       if the returned plan has equal or worse cost than the initial plan,
       the score for that task is 0. Otherwise, a plan of cost C scores
       log(G/C)/log(G/C*), where G is the cost of the initial plan and C*
       the cost of the best-known plan; the score of a planner is the sum
       of its scores over all tasks.

 - hard-to-ground:
       instances that cannot be grounded with state-of-the-art grounding
       approaches, i.e., whose fully instantiated representation is too large
       to be computed within reasonable time and memory. The score of a
       planner on a solved task is C*/C, where C is the cost of the cheapest
       plan it discovered and C* is the cost of the best-known plan. The score
       of a planner is the sum of its scores over all tasks. For this track,
       the input PDDL fragment is restricted: no axioms, no conditional
       effects, and no quantifiers.

In all tracks, if a planner returns an invalid plan in a domain, all tasks of
that domain count as unsolved; if this happens in more than one domain, the
entry is disqualified.

A competing team may participate in any track, so competitors are strongly
encouraged to participate in every track supported by their planners. Details
regarding the rules and evaluation criteria of all tracks can be found at the
competition website.


Registration
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Registration is expected to open in late January / February 2027. Potential
participants are requested to send us an email expressing interest
(ipc2027-classical@googlegroups.com), providing:

 1. names of participants,
 2. e-mail contacts,
 3. GitHub usernames,
 4. the number of repositories (code bases) the team needs (multiple
    planners can be built from the same repository), and
 5. a (tentative) list of the tracks the team intends to enter.

Note that each person may be listed as an author of at most three planners per
track (see "Planner submission" below).

Based on this, we will create private repositories under the ipc2027-classical
GitHub organization and add all team members with write access. Exact
registration instructions will be announced on the mailing list and on the
website.


Planner submission
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

As in previous editions, competitors must submit the source code of their
planners, which will be run by the organizers on the actual competition
domains/problems, unknown to the competitors until that time. This way no
fine-tuning of the planners will be possible.

We will use the container technology Apptainer (essentially a fork of
Singularity) to promote reproducibility. Planners are developed in the team's
private repository; we only consider the branch "ipc2027-classical", and every
file named Apptainer.<shortname> in its root directory defines one
competition entry, which may participate in multiple tracks (declared via
the Tracks label of the recipe). After the competition, we plan to make all
planners, domains, and related data publicly available.

To keep the required computation time manageable, each person may be listed
as an author of at most three planners per track. Please also limit your
submissions to one variant per planner: if two submissions would be described
by two separate planner abstracts, they count as two planners; variants that
can be described in one abstract count as one planner.

After the feature stop (see the schedule below), only bug-fix pull requests
are accepted. We will then run all planners on all tasks and give
participants access to the logs and results of their own planners, and we
ask you to check your results and send pull requests with detailed bug fixes
if something went wrong on your side. We plan at least two rounds of this
testing and debugging.


Planner abstract
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

All competitors must submit an abstract (max. 300 words) and an up to 4-page
paper describing their planners. After the competition, we ask the
participants to analyze the results of their planners and submit an extended
version of their paper. An important requirement for IPC 2027 competitors is
to give the organizers the right to post their paper and the source code of
their planners on the official IPC 2027 website; the source code must be
released under a license allowing free non-commercial use.
